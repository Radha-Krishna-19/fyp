Combining BFDH and Wulff

One rule that blends the geometric (BFDH) and energetic (Wulff) shape methods, what it gives for four MOFs, why no best weight is claimed, and the notebook that runs it on any crystal file.

Step 15 of 19 · next: Experiment

1What This Page Does

You know
Two ways to turn a MOF into a shape: BFDH from the face spacing (Morphology) and Wulff from the face energies (Phase 4).
This page adds
One rule that blends the two, what it gives for four MOFs, and the notebook that runs it on any crystal file.
Which lets us
Ask whether the blend is better, worse or the same as either method alone, which was the team's question for the guide review.

In simple words

There are two ways to guess the outside shape of a crystal. The first (BFDH) says that faces whose layers are far apart are slow to grow, so they end up large. It looks only at the geometry. The second (Wulff) says that the faces that cost the least energy to create end up large. It looks at the chemistry. The idea is to blend them with a dial: at 0 we use only the geometry, at 1 only the energy, and in between a mixture. We then see whether the mixture gives a shape closer to the one seen in the laboratory.

In technical words

Each face family f has a distance to the centre of the crystal Rf and the shape is the convex region {x : nf·x ≤ Rf}. BFDH gives Rf ∝ 1/deff and Wulff gives Rf ∝ γf. Both are scaled so that the smallest value is 1, so neither dominates because of its units. The blend is the weighted geometric mean

Rf(w) = RBFDH,f1−w · RWulff,fw,   0 ≤ w ≤ 1.

Taking logarithms, ln Rf(w) = (1−w) ln RBFDH + w ln RWulff, so the blend moves along a straight line between the two methods in log space, and w = 0 and w = 1 give exactly the two original shapes. The same list of faces is used for both so that the comparison is fair. The set of face families and their energies come from the same pipeline as Phase 4 (UFF, unrelaxed, flat bond cost).

BFDH makes {111} the closest face (R = 1) and {100} further out, so {111} is large and the crystal is a truncated octahedron. Wulff reverses it: {100} is the cheapest face and {111} lies further out. At w = 0.5 the ratio R111/R100 passes through an intermediate value and the shape is a truncated cube; the name for each w is in the table below.

  • Blending adds nothing for HKUST-1, ZIF-8. BFDH and Wulff already give the same shape, so every blend does too.
  • It matters for MOF-5 and UiO-66. There the two methods disagree and the blend passes through intermediate shapes. This is a transition, not a new shape.
  • Against the additive-free crystals of the experimental benchmark (Experiment), Wulff (w = 1) matches MOF-5, ZIF-8 and BFDH (w = 0) matches ZIF-8. HKUST-1 is a truncated octahedron in every column while the crystals are octahedra, so the dominant {111} face is right but the shape name is not. For UiO-66 the additive-free records report poorly faceted cubes, which no column gives; the octahedron at w ≥ 0.5 is the habit grown with modulators.
The dial w is a free parameter and there are four MOFs. Picking the weight that scores best would fit it to the test set, so the table shows all five values and no ranking. The honest reading today is that the blend has not been shown to beat either method. To show that, w must be chosen on some MOFs and tested on others, and the energies must first be checked by DFT. If the blend does win on held-out MOFs, that would be a new model worth writing up; today it is an idea with a working implementation.

3The Notebook: Any Crystal File

The Colab notebook notebooks/morphograph_mof/MorphoGraph_hybrid.ipynb (generated by code/morphology/tools/make_nb.py) runs the whole chain on any CIF that contains a unit cell, with no machine-learning model and no GPU:

  1. Upload the CIF in the upload cell (any crystal system; a file without a cell is refused).
  2. Set NAME so the plot titles carry the right name.
  3. The notebook finds the symmetry, lists the allowed faces, computes the UFF energy of each face family with all atoms including hydrogen, and draws the BFDH, blended and Wulff shapes.
  4. An optional cell accepts energies from another source (for example CHGNet or DFT) and recomputes the same shapes from them.

The notebook differs from Keerthi's CHGNet notebook: it uses the force-field energies and needs seconds, whereas the CHGNet notebook relaxes slabs and needs a GPU for hours. Its limits are those of Phase 4 (no solvent, no charges, no temperature).
