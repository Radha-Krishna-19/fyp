In simple words

Before the panel we described each MOF by one number from how its building blocks are connected. After the panel we predicted the outside shape of its crystals. Here we ask three questions. If we build small MOF crystals of different shapes, does that number change? Does it change between the shapes our different methods predict? And across many real MOFs, does the number tell us anything about the predicted shape?

In technical words

Every earlier spectrum was measured on a torus (the labelled quotient graph tiled with wrap-around), which has no surface and no shape. Here the quotient graph is tiled without wrap-around, every block is placed at its Cartesian centroid, and the graph is cut to a finite particle: a box of unit cells, or a polyhedron {x : nf·x ≤ s Rf} with the face distances Rf of a morphology prediction. Δα uses the settings of the full bands: top 20% of blocks by degree as influential, radii to half the diameter, 20 trials, seeds 0–4. Error bars are the seed spread only.

Result. At equal block count every compact shape lies within -0.044 to +0.050 of the cube trend, which is the same size as the scatter of the cubes about their own trend (0.043), and the order of the shapes changes between 2000 and 4000 blocks. Compared at equal diameter they differ by up to +0.32, but only because an octahedron has a much smaller graph diameter than a cube of the same N (in a pcu net, graph distance behaves like the taxicab distance, and the octahedron is the taxicab ball). So the spectrum does not tell apart the compact shapes our methods predict for MOF-5. Test 1 showed that it does tell apart flat and elongated particles.

Result. The raw links are real (Δα against aspect ratio ρ = +0.50, p = 0.0003), but with graph size held fixed they vanish (ρ = +0.001, p = 0.99). Size explains them: Δα rises with node count (ρ = +0.80) and more stretched predicted shapes come with bigger periodic graphs (sphericity against node count ρ = -0.48). The periodic Δα of the first half carries no information about predicted shape beyond size.

0What Carries Over From the First Half

QuestionAnswerEvidence
Does Δα of a finite particle depend on how flat or long it is?Yes, for MOF-5, beyond sizeTest 1
Does it separate compact shapes (cube vs octahedron vs truncated octahedron)?No; differences are within the method's own scatterTest 2
Does the periodic Δα predict a MOF's crystal shape?No, once graph size is controlledTest 3

What carries over is the graph (the labelled quotient graph, used by the morphology work in Phase 3) and the habit of controlling size. The spectrum itself becomes useful for shape only when it is measured on a finite particle, and then only for anisotropy (plate, rod). A sensible next test is the cell-deformation shapes of Cell Deform, which are plates and rods.

Tests 1 and 2 use one structure (MOF-5): it is the only demonstration MOF whose file holds an orthogonal conventional cell and whose block graph is connected. A block belongs to a particle if its centroid is inside the shape, so surfaces are a staircase one block high. BFDH shape names for low-symmetry crystals use cubic-style labels and are not used in any statistic; only sphericity and aspect ratio are.
