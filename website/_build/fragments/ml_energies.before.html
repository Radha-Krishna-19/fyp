0The Short Answer

The team notebook of 14 November computes surface energies with a machine-learned model (CHGNet) and uses them to draw crystal shapes and to train a neural network. For 6 crystal faces in 4 MOFs it reported a negative surface energy. A negative surface energy would mean that the crystal gains energy by breaking itself apart, so a stable crystal with those faces could not exist, and the program correctly refused to draw a shape for those MOFs.

The arithmetic is not wrong. The negative values come from where the water used to cap the cut surface is taken from. The notebook takes it from a single water molecule at absolute zero, which is the most favourable water there can be. Taking it instead from liquid water at room temperature, using standard thermochemical tables, raises every surface energy, and none of the 109 relaxed surfaces is negative any more (Section 4). Two further problems were found and fixed on the way: a capping rule that puts two protons on one oxygen, and two earlier fixes (K3 and K11) that were missing from this version of the notebook.

Correcting the water reference removes an impossible result; it does not make the energies right. The machine-learned energies have not been checked against quantum chemistry (DFT), and relaxation still lowers them by a large fraction. The shapes on this page are therefore model results for a stated water condition, not predictions to be trusted on their own.

0Background: What Was Already Known

This section is established science and the team's earlier design. Nothing in it is a new result.

What a surface energy is

Cutting a crystal along a plane breaks the bonds that cross the plane. The energy this costs, per unit area of new surface, is the surface energy γ (J/m²). Because a cut makes two surfaces, γ is half the cost of the cut per unit area. A face with a low γ is cheap to expose, grows slowly and ends up large; the Wulff construction turns the set of γ values into a crystal shape (Learn: surface energy). For a stable crystal every γ is positive: if one were negative, the crystal would lower its energy by splitting into ever thinner pieces, and no finite shape would exist.

The team's pipeline (steps 1–8)

  1. Standardise the CIF file and find its symmetry.
  2. Split it into building blocks (metal nodes and organic linkers).
  3. List the face families and the different depths at which each face can be cut (terminations).
  4. Cut a slab, a crystal a few nanometres thick with empty space above and below.
  5. Cap every broken metal–linker bond with water (below).
  6. Compute energies with CHGNet, a neural-network model trained on quantum-chemistry data (Deng et al., 2023), first without moving the atoms (static), then after letting them move to lower energy (relaxed).
  7. Check them with a second model, MACE-MP-0 (Batatia et al., 2023), and prepare inputs for DFT.
  8. Build the shape (Wulff construction).

A graph neural network (GNN) is then trained to predict the relaxed γ of a slab from its structure, so that new MOFs would not need the slow relaxation.

Why cut bonds are capped with water

A bare cut leaves metal atoms missing a neighbour and linker oxygens with a dangling bond. Real MOF crystals grow in a solvent, and their surfaces are covered by solvent, water or hydroxide, not left bare. The pipeline mimics this: each broken metal–oxygen bond is closed by one water molecule split into two pieces, OH⁻ on the metal and H⁺ on the oxygen (dissociative or heterolytic capping). The atoms of n water molecules are therefore added to a slab with n broken bonds.

The energy of the added water, and why it matters

The surface energy subtracts what the slab's atoms would have cost anyway, inside the crystal and in the water they came from:

γ = [ Eslab − N ebulk − n μH₂O ] / 2A(1)

where

Eslab
energy of the capped slab;
N, ebulk
number of framework atoms in the slab and the energy per atom of the bulk crystal;
n
number of water molecules used as caps;
μH₂O
the energy of one water molecule in the place it came from: its chemical potential;
A
area of one face of the slab (two faces, hence 2A).

An everyday picture: the chemical potential is the price of water. If water costs nothing, covering a surface with it is free and any attraction between water and the surface is pure gain. If water is dear, because it has to be pulled out of liquid water or humid air where it is comfortable, the gain is smaller. The notebook sets μH₂O = EH₂O, the energy of one isolated molecule at absolute zero, with no thermal motion and no entropy. That is the cheapest water there can be. Under any real condition the price is lower by Δμ (a negative number), and

γ(Δμ) = γ0 K − n Δμ / 2A(2)

This is the standard way surface energies are put in contact with a gas or liquid reservoir (ab initio thermodynamics; Reuter and Scheffler, Phys. Rev. B 65, 035406, 2001). Equation (2) is a straight line in Δμ whose slope, n/2A, is the number of caps per unit area: faces with many caps per area move most.

The price of water under real conditions

Δμ = [H(T) − H(0)] − T S(T) + kT ln(p/1 bar), with the enthalpy and entropy of water vapour from the NIST-JANAF tables (table H-064: H(298.15 K) − H(0) = 9.904 kJ/mol, S = 188.834 J/mol/K; at 400 K, H − H(298.15) = 3.452 kJ/mol and S = 198.788 J/mol/K) and, for liquid water, the CODATA Gibbs energies of formation of the gas and the liquid (−228.582 and −237.129 kJ/mol). Zero-point energy is left out of both the free molecule and the caps, as is usual in this method; the two partly cancel, and the residual, of order 0.1 eV per molecule, is the uncertainty of every Δμ below.

0Three Causes

Cause 1: the water is taken from a free molecule at 0 K (all six slabs)

In equation (1) the water caps are paid for at the price of a free molecule at absolute zero. On a cut surface the metal atoms have lost a neighbour, and a water-derived cap binds to them strongly. With water this cheap, the binding gained outweighs the cost of cutting the crystal, and γ falls below zero. This affects every slab, but most strongly those with many caps per unit area: the slope n/2A of the 109 relaxed slabs runs from 0.067 to 0.795 J/m² per eV (median 0.261). At liquid water (C, Δμ = −0.570 eV) γ rises by between 0.04 and 0.45 J/m², which is larger than many of the relaxed values themselves. The water reference is therefore not a small correction: it decides the order of the faces for several MOFs (Section 4).

Cause 2: two fixes were missing (Mg-MOF-74 and HKUST-1)

On 10 November two problems in the pipeline were found and fixed (The morphology map, problems K3 and K11). K11: after the bulk relaxation the cell is written to a file and read back, which re-orients it, so faces of crystals whose working cell differs from the conventional cell were converted to the wrong planes, or the program fell back to the unrelaxed cell. K3: slab and bulk energies could then come from different geometries. The notebook of 14 November does not contain either fix. For Mg-MOF-74 {2̅10} it cut its slab from the unrelaxed conventional cell (378 atoms, static γ 1.216, relaxed −0.0135 J/m²); with both fixes the slab is cut from the relaxed working cell (126 atoms, static 1.016, relaxed +0.002 J/m²; results_full_fixed/fix_Mg-MOF-74.log). Both are effectively zero: the missing fixes change the static value by 0.2 J/m² but are not what makes this face negative. The same holds for ZnBDC {001}, whose working cell is its conventional cell but whose slab was still built from the unrelaxed atoms: with the fixes its static γ falls from 2.297 to 0.663 J/m², while the relaxed value stays at −0.028 J/m² (table at the end of this section). Relaxation removes most of the K3 error; the water reference remains.

Cause 3: a capping rule that puts two protons on one oxygen (K12, new)

The capping rule adds one H⁺ to a linker oxygen for every metal bond it lost. In MOF-74 and some other frameworks an oxygen bridges two metal atoms; if the cut removes both, the oxygen receives two protons and becomes C–OH₂⁺, a group that is not stable (panel 4 of the figure above). Among the 118 slabs of the 16 relaxed MOFs, 26 contain such a group, in Cd3BTB2, Mg-MOF-74, Ni-DOBDC. ZnBDC and MIL-47 are not affected, so this is not why they are negative. Cd3BTB2 is: with the corrected capping (and the restored fixes) its {010} slab relaxes to a positive γ even with the old water reference (table below), so for Cd3BTB2 the capping error, not only the water reference, made the value negative. During relaxation CHGNet moves such protons back: in the fixed run of Mg-MOF-74 {2̅10} the six OH caps became three OH caps, two water molecules on magnesium and one water molecule that left the surface altogether. The rule is now fixed: a linker oxygen receives at most one proton, and each surplus proton turns the nearest metal OH cap into a water molecule bound to the metal. The atoms added, the charge balance and n are unchanged.

A water molecule that has left the surface is not part of it. At a realistic Δμ it would leave, so for these slabs equation (2) is applied with n reduced by the number of free molecules found after relaxation.

  • K1, shape names. Names now come from the exact limits of the cubic map: a pure cube, octahedron or rhombic dodecahedron only within numerical noise (0.1 % of the area), the cuboctahedron only within 0.005 of its exact {100} share 6/(6 + 2√3). All 14 test ratios of checks/naming.py are named correctly (the notebook version misnamed 3).
  • K2, the metal rod of MOF-74. Two metals bonded to the same linker oxygen now belong to one inorganic unit, so the Mg–O rod of Mg-MOF-74 (and of Ni-DOBDC) is recognised as one infinite unit, and Cd3BTB2 gets its Cd3 cluster; the other eight MOFs checked are unchanged. With static energies only, the Mg-MOF-74 shape is still a block (block/prism: {2̅10} 45%, {101} 41%, {100} 14% at liquid water); with relaxed energies from the GPU run it becomes a hexagonal rod along c, as in the laboratory (Section 5).
  • K5–K10, six smaller problems, fixed in the corrected notebook after the GPU run: the UiO-66 input now has its Zr6O4(OH)4 node; an open Wulff shape is called open, not a rod; faces are ranked by the effective spacing d/n, so faces forbidden by symmetry are not kept ahead of allowed ones (for the four benchmark MOFs the kept faces do not change); the MACE rank correlation is also taken over all slabs; the DFT inputs get the surface dipole correction and a stated spin state; and a cell checks one face at three slab thicknesses. Their numbers need the next GPU run.
  • K4, a wrong reference shape. The notebook compared UiO-66 with octahedra; additive-free UiO-66 gives cubes in our records, and octahedra need benzoic or acetic acid. Corrected in the notebook's input table.

0What Changes for the GNN

This section is written from the machine-learning side. The GNN learns relaxed γ from the unrelaxed slab structure. Its output passes through a softplus function, so it can only predict positive values, yet the notebook trained it on the 6 negative labels as if they were real. A model cannot fit a label it cannot output, and the label itself was an artefact of the water reference. The corrected mofgnn.py moves every label (relaxed and static, used for pretraining) to a stated water condition with at_water_mu(), and load_dataset() now drops any label ≤ 0 and prints which ones it dropped.

The GPU run repeated the cross-validation with the corrected labels (liquid water, labels ≤ 0 dropped): each of the 16 MOFs is held out in turn and predicted by models trained on the others.

0What It Means, Its Limits and the Possibilities

What can be said

  • The negative surface energies were an artefact of the water reference, not a property of the MOFs. With water taken from any realistic condition, every relaxed γ is positive.
  • The water condition is a real input to the model, not a detail: for several MOFs it changes the order of the faces and hence the predicted shape. Any shape from this pipeline must say which water condition it assumes.
  • The capping rule had a chemistry error (K12) for bridging oxygens, now fixed; the K3 and K11 fixes must be carried into every new version of the notebook.

What cannot be said

  • That the corrected energies are right. Relaxation still lowers them by 60–100 %, no value has been compared with DFT, and the MACE-MP-0 check re-scores the CHGNet geometries rather than relaxing them independently, so it tests the energy model but not the geometry.
  • That liquid water is the right condition. MOFs are often grown in DMF or other solvents, with modulators; water is the pipeline's stand-in for whatever covers the surface.
  • That the free-energy terms of the caps are negligible. Their vibrations and entropy are left out (Section 1), an uncertainty of about 0.1 eV per molecule in Δμ, that is up to 0.08 J/m² in γ for the most densely capped slab.

The possibilities, and what would decide between them

  • Water binds as a molecule, not split. On Mg-MOF-74 relaxation turned split caps back into bound water. A capping scheme with whole water molecules, compared with the split scheme at the same Δμ, would show which the model prefers; DFT on one slab would decide.
  • A different solvent. Capping with DMF or a carboxylate modulator, with its own chemical potential, is the same method with a different reservoir, and is the natural way to model additives.
  • The bare surface is the stable one at low Δμ. The true γ(Δμ) is the lowest of all possible coverings; comparing capped and bare slabs, as in Section 3, gives the crossover.
  • What would disprove the correction: a DFT calculation of one of these slabs giving a negative γ at liquid-water Δμ, or a converged CHGNet value that changes sign with slab thickness.
