0The Map

A cubic crystal bounded by {100}, {110} and {111} faces has a Wulff shape that depends only on two ratios, x = γ110/γ100 and y = γ111/γ100, because multiplying every energy by one factor only scales the crystal. A family appears only if its planes cut off a corner of the shape the other families make, which gives the exact boundaries below. They are derived step by step, with a clickable version of the map, in Step 12 of From a CIF to a shape; this page uses them.

0Conditions on the Map: Three Tests

Theory. By the Gibbs adsorption equation a face's energy falls as an additive that binds it becomes more concentrated, dγF = −ΓF dμ. An additive that binds one family lowers only that family's energy, so the crystal's point moves steadily toward that family's corner of the map, through a fixed order of shapes, and never turns back. That order is testable.

Test 1: laboratory series (series_test.py). Every controlled series in the dataset with an increasing dose, ratio or time was placed on the map. 6 series change shape on the map, and all 6 move one way along one axis. If the same shapes were listed in random order, the chance that all 6 would be this orderly is 2.7 × 10−4. This number is conditional on two choices, stated here: it counts only the 6 of 10 series whose shapes all lie on the map, and its null assumes every order of a series' shapes is equally likely (for three shapes, 2 of the 6 orders are monotone, so 1/3 per series). Shape names are the papers' own.

Plates, spheres, needles and intergrown crystals come from growth kinetics; no Wulff model can describe them.

Test 2: conditions as labels (condition_model.py). The dominant face of 82 cubic-MOF records from 25 papers was predicted with each paper held out in turn; the interval is a 95 % bootstrap over papers.

ModelAll recordsNo additive (35)With additive (47)
M0: most common face overall43 % (28–61)49 %38 %

On additive-free records the structure-only prediction (M2) is as good as knowing the usual answer for that MOF (M1). Adding the additive as a label (M3) makes the records with additives worse, because the same additive type moves different MOFs different ways (monocarboxylic acids push UiO-66 toward octahedra and HKUST-1 toward cubes) and papers report doses in incompatible units. A model of conditions must therefore predict the move from computed binding energies and one dose unit, not from labels.

How strong is that difference? On the 47 records with an additive, M2 is right where M3 is wrong in 15 records and the reverse in 5 (exact McNemar test, p = 0.041); resampling whole papers gives a 95 % interval of +0.02 to +0.50 for M2’s advantage in accuracy. The difference is real but only just: it rests on 17 papers, and its lower end is close to zero.

Test 3: a modulator cap, first attempt (modulator_caps.py). The lowest water-capped slab of each face was recapped with formate and scored without relaxation. The binding energy per site, εF for {100} / {110} / {111}, is -1.27 / -1.36 / -1.43 eV for HKUST-1 and -1.35 / -1.31 / -1.27 eV for ZIF-8, which moves HKUST-1 toward octahedra and ZIF-8 toward cubes. For UiO-66 two formates crowd each Zr and the result (-0.10 / 0.39 / 8.23 eV) is not physical. With unrelaxed caps, this shows only that the pipeline can compute a direction; it is not a result.

15 of the 16 polyhedral lab shapes for these MOFs are generated (not generated: ZIF-8 truncated cube, which needs two faces lowered together). 0Checking the Team's CIF-to-Wulff Pipeline (Steps 1–8)

Kshitij's notebook MOF_Wulff_steps1-8.ipynb goes from a CIF to a Wulff shape: standardise the structure, split it into building blocks, list face families and terminations, cut slabs of whole blocks, cap every broken metal–linker bond with dissociated water, compute CHGNet energies, and build and name the shape. It answers three weaknesses of the October CHGNet run (slabs cut through blocks, uncapped bonds, no DFT plan). It was run unchanged on its four input CIFs on a 2-core CPU with 8 GB of memory (code/morphology/steps1_8_check/).

Reading. ZIF-8 agrees with the lab in both modes (relaxed: a rhombic dodecahedron, all {110}), as every earlier model did. 0What This Shows, and Its Limits

  • Shown: from a unit cell the map generates a set of morphologies in a predicted order, and every laboratory series that changes shape on the map follows that order. This is the panel's request, with a test.
  • Shown: the additive-free shape is right where it is an equilibrium shape (ZIF-8), right in direction but not far enough for HKUST-1, and depends on the model for MOF-5.
  • Not shown: shapes set by growth kinetics (plates, rods, intergrown crystals) are outside any Wulff model. The map covers cubic crystals only; Mg-MOF-74 needs a non-cubic version after K2 is fixed.
  • Not shown: no condition model yet beats the structure-only prediction on records with additives (Test 2), and the first computed modulator energies are not physical for UiO-66.
  • Data limit: no database lists the morphologies a given CIF can form. Ours is collected by hand from papers (134 records, about 12 MOFs, doses in incompatible units): enough to test a method, not to train a general model. This question was put to the guide on 10 November.
