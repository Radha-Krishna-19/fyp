1The Paper We Built On — and Exactly What We Used From It

The network half of this project follows a published method. This section states plainly what the paper provides, which parts we used, which part we initially got wrong, and what we added.

Used directly, unchanged

Every equation above: the box-growing measure, the partition function, the mass exponent, the Legendre transform, the generalized dimension, and the asymmetry metric. code_11_nmfa_paper.py implements them as written, and each function carries the paper's own formula in its docstring.

What we initially got wrong

We used box-covering — randomly tiling the network — where the paper specifies box-growing. It explicitly contrasts the two. Box-covering is stochastic, which is why an earlier version needed 40 repeat trials to stabilise. The published measure is deterministic: three runs give α₀ = 1.630821 every time.
What we added that the paper does not cover. The paper analyses social, biological and synthetic networks, where nodes are all different. A crystal is not like that. We contribute: (i) a chemically correct, periodic route from a .cif to the block network — the object the method needs as input; (ii) supercell expansion, because a primitive cell of 7 blocks has no range of radii to fit a power law over; and (iii) the finding that iNMFA is degenerate on a defect-free crystal, since the influential blocks are symmetry-equivalent and share one growth curve. That is a genuine boundary condition on the method, not a failure of it.

2The Multifractal Spectrum — the Family Fingerprint

This is the analysis the brief actually asked for: highlight the influential nodes, compute a spectrum, and test whether frameworks of one family fall inside a common band. The spectrum here is not the Laplacian eigenvalues of Section 4 — it is the multifractal singularity spectrum f(α), obtained by covering the network with boxes of increasing radius and measuring how the box sizes seen by the influential nodes scale.

How it is computed. Cover the block graph with boxes of radius r; each influential node inherits the size of the box covering it, giving a probability measure pi(r). The partition function Z(q,r) = Σ piq scales as rτ(q); a log–log fit gives τ(q), and a Legendre transform gives α(q) = dτ/dq and f(α) = qα − τ. A simple fractal would give a single point; a multifractal gives a curve, and the width of that curve says how unevenly connectivity is distributed across the framework.

What had to be corrected to get a spectrum at all

The method is sound; the first implementation returned an undefined spectrum for half the test structures. Each fix below is a specific, checkable defect.

Does this satisfy the brief? The method does: influential nodes are identified, a spectrum is computed, a reference band is built, and a candidate is scored against it — and unrelated frameworks land outside the band while family members land inside. What is not yet established is the family claim. There is exactly one zirconium structure here, so "all Zr-MOFs occupy this band" remains a demonstration of mechanism rather than a result. It needs a few hundred MOFs grouped by metal, from CoRE MOF or QMOF — which is what the original notebook was set up to download, and the analysis code runs per-CIF so it batches unchanged.

3Against the Published Method — Box-Growing

The method comes from Xiao et al., "Deciphering the generating rules and functionalities of complex networks", Scientific Reports 11, 22964 (2021). Reading it closely revealed that our implementation — and the original notebook — used the wrong underlying measure.

Box-covering is not box-growing. The paper proposes box-growing and explicitly contrasts it with the box-counting family. The difference decides everything downstream:
What the measure isRandom?
box-covering (what we had)tile the whole network with randomly-seeded boxes; a node's measure is the size of whichever box happens to cover ityes — needs many trials
box-growing (the paper)centre a box on node i and grow its radius; the measure is Mi(r), the number of nodes within distance rno — fully deterministic
Because the published measure is deterministic, the reproducibility problem that forced 40 box-covering trials does not exist in the correct method. Repeated runs are bit-identical.
The published quantities, now computed. ui(r) = Mi(r)/M · Uq(r) = Σ uiq · Uq ~ (r/d)τ(q) · α = dτ/dq · f(α) = qα − τ · generalized dimension D(q) = τ(q)/q · asymmetry A = ln[(α₀−αmin)/(αmax−α₀)] with α₀ at the maximum of f. Per the paper: width w is structural heterogeneity, α₀ is structural complexity, A>0 means frequent structures dominate ("clump"), A<0 means rare ones do ("thorn").
